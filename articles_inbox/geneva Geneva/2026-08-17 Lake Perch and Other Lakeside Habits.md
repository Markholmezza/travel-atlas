Geneva sits where the Rhône leaves Lac Léman, and a surprising share of daily life in the city happens at the water's edge: swimming off the jetties in summer, crossing the harbour by boat, and eating fish that, in theory, came out of the lake.

## Filets de perche

The classic lakeside dish is *filets de perche*: small perch fillets, pan-fried in butter and served with a lemon or herb butter sauce and a plate of fries. It is on the menu of almost every café along the shore. Perch from the lake itself is caught in limited quantities, so much of what is served is imported; restaurants that use local fish tend to say so, and charge accordingly.

## Swimming in the city

The Bains des Pâquis, a public bathing area on a jetty on the right bank, is open all year. In summer it is packed with swimmers; in winter it serves fondue and runs a sauna for anyone who still wants a cold dip. The water is clear enough to see the stones on the bottom from the jetty.

## Crossing the harbour

The small yellow boats known as *Mouettes* shuttle across the harbour between the two banks every few minutes. They are part of the public transport network, so a regular ticket covers the crossing, and they are the most pleasant way to get from one side of the city to the other, with a close view of the Jet d'Eau on the way.
